Administrator guide

Administrators can do everything a technician can, plus manage user accounts, delete installations and review the audit trail. Read the Technician guide for working with installations; this page covers what is specific to administrators.

Signing in

  1. Open SolarCORE and click Log in Enter your username and password, or use Continue with Google if it is set up.
  2. Enter the code A 6-digit code is emailed to your account. It is valid for 10 minutes and allows 5 tries. If it does not arrive, request a new one after 60 seconds.
  3. You are in The dashboard opens.

The first time the built-in admin account signs in, SolarCORE asks for a new password and a real email address before anything else. See First sign-in.

Staying signed in After 30 minutes without activity you are signed out. A warning appears shortly before; click Stay signed in to continue. To sign out yourself, open the menu under your name (top right) and choose Log Out.

Users

Open Administration → Users. The page has five sections, listed down the left side:

SectionHolds
Admins and TechniciansStaff accounts. Create new staff accounts here.
ClientsApproved client accounts, with the installations each one owns.
Pending ApprovalPeople who registered themselves and are waiting for a decision.
DeclinedRegistrations you declined. They can still be approved later.
ArchivedDeactivated accounts put away, both staff and clients.

Create a staff account

  1. Open Admins and Technicians and start a new staff account.
  2. Fill in first and last name, username, email, a temporary password (at least 8 characters) and the role: Administrator or Technician.
  3. Click Create account. Give the person their username and temporary password in person or by phone, not in a group chat.

Approve or decline a client

Clients sign up with Create account on the sign-in page. Their account cannot sign in until you approve it.

  1. Open Pending Approval. The bell also tells you about new registrations.
  2. Approve a real customer, or decline one you do not recognise. Declined registrations can be approved later with Approve after all, or deleted.
  3. Link their installations. Edit each installation they own and choose their account as the Owner. They then see it under My Installations and can report problems.

Edit, deactivate, archive

  • Edit changes name, username, email, role or password. Leave the password blank to keep the current one.
  • Deactivate stops an account from signing in, immediately. Reactivate restores it.
  • Archive moves a deactivated account to the Archived section, to keep the lists short. Its records stay; Unarchive brings it back.
  • Delete is only possible for staff accounts that never created or changed a record. Otherwise deactivate them, which keeps the audit trail intact. Client accounts cannot be deleted, only deactivated.

Safeguards

To keep at least one way in, SolarCORE refuses to:

  • change your own role or status, or archive or delete your own account;
  • demote, deactivate or delete the last active administrator;
  • change a client account's role (clients keep the Client role);
  • archive an account that is still active.

Deleting installations

Only administrators can delete an installation. In Installations, use the delete icon on its row and confirm. The record disappears from the list, the map, analytics and exports, and the deletion is recorded in the audit trail.

Audit Trail

Open Administration → Audit Trail. It records who did what and when:

  • sign-ins, and blocked or failed sign-ins;
  • installations added, changed (with the before and after values) and deleted;
  • user accounts created, changed, approved, declined, deactivated and archived;
  • imports and exports (CSV, GeoJSON and PDF);
  • client report and inquiry updates.

Filter by area (module) and by user. The list shows 50 entries per page, newest first.

Entries are kept for two years. Older entries are removed by the purge script your server administrator runs (see Deployment), and the purge records itself in the trail.

Inquiries and client reports

Administrators handle these exactly like technicians. See Technician guide → Client Desk.
