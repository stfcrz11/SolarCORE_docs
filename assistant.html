Using SolarCORE

Sol, the assistant

Sol answers questions about the installation records in plain language. After signing in, open it with the round Sol button at the bottom right of any page.

The chat window

  • Sol's replies appear on the left, yours on the right in gold, each with the time it was sent.
  • Quick questions sit above the message box. Click one to ask it. If they do not all fit, scroll the row sideways: swipe on a phone, or use the mouse wheel (a thin scrollbar appears when you point at the row).
  • The – button at the top right minimises the window. The conversation stays while you move between pages, until you sign out.
  • Figures come back easy to scan: one per line, with the key number in bold.
  • To hide the Sol button, turn off Show Sol in Settings.

  • Typos are fine. "hw mny instllations in isabla" is read as "how many installations in Isabela". Place names are corrected only to places in your own records.
  • Filipino and Taglish work too, e.g. "Ilan ang off-grid sa Isabela?"
  • Follow-ups work. Sol remembers the last few exchanges, so "how about Cauayan?" or "only the hybrid ones" continue the previous question.
  • Answer its offers. Sol often ends with one offer, such as "Want me to break that down by system type?" Reply yes, sure, sige or go ahead to get it, or no thanks to move on.
  • Questions without a ready-made answer are answered from SolarCORE's reference text and the current figures, and Sol says plainly what those cannot tell. Questions unrelated to SolarCORE or solar energy are politely declined.

Why its numbers can be trusted

Language models are good with words and bad with numbers. They can state a wrong figure with confidence. SolarCORE therefore splits the work:

  1. Understand. The model works out what you are asking: a count, a capacity or a list, and for which place, type or status.
  2. Query. SolarCORE runs a normal database query for the figures. Place names are matched against places that really exist in the records, so a misspelt or invented place is reported as unknown rather than answered with a confident zero.
  3. Word. The model turns those figures into a sentence. It is given the numbers; it does not calculate them.

Installed capacity follows the same rule as everywhere else: systems that are awarded or under construction are not counted as installed.

Who sees what

  • Staff get figures for the whole company.
  • Clients get figures for their own installations only. Their account decides this, not their question, so asking "show me all installations" does not widen it. If a client asks about a place where they have no installations, the answer is that they have none there.
  • Questions about which sites are near each other are answered for staff only. Account figures are for administrators only.
  • Before anything is sent to the AI service, installation names, people's names, email addresses and phone numbers are replaced with codes such as [S1], and put back on SolarCORE's own server when the reply returns. Owner names are left out of what the AI words altogether.
  • For follow-ups, the AI service sees your earlier questions and a one-line summary of each answer (counts and filters only), never the earlier replies themselves.
  • Please do not type phone numbers, addresses or other personal details into the chat; Sol does not need them.

What Sol cannot do

Sol only looks things up and explains them. It cannot export, download, email, print, add, edit, archive or approve anything. Ask it to, and it says so and shows you where to do it yourself:

Example You: csv download
Sol: I can't make an export for you, but here's how to do it: open the Installations tab and use the Export button above the table…

When the AI service is off or busy

Sol keeps working without the AI service: if it is switched off (AI_ENABLED=false), has no key, or cannot be reached. It falls back to built-in answers for the common questions; the wording is plainer but the figures are the same. See Configuration → AI assistant.

On Groq's free tier, the AI service accepts only so much text per minute. If several questions are asked quickly, Sol waits a few seconds and tries again, and if that is not enough it gives the plain answer instead. See Troubleshooting.

Limits

  • Lists show up to 8 installations at a time, and say how many there are in total ("showing 8 of 140"). For a full list, use the Installations page and its filters.
  • It knows only what is in SolarCORE, not, for example, live energy production.
  • The figures are always the database's, but the AI's wording around them can occasionally be off, for example in a comparison. When a number matters, check it on the page.
