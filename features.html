Getting started

Features

A tour of what SolarCORE does. Each section links to the page that explains it in detail.

Interactive map

  • Every installation as a pin, coloured by type: on-grid, off-grid or hybrid.
  • Basemaps: Satellite + labels, Satellite and Street.
  • Layers for staff: Pins, Cluster (nearby pins grouped into a counted bubble), Heatmap (density) and Coverage (a circle around each site).
  • Filters by type and status, with a "Showing N of M installations" count.
  • Search, zoom, and a compass button that flies to where installations are densest.
  • Click a pin for its details and photos. Staff can click an empty spot to add an installation there.
  • Proximity search: installations near a given site, from 500 m to 10 km, measured with PostGIS.
  • Public map for visitors: an anonymous heatmap of operational sites, never exact locations.

More: The map.

Installation records

  • Name, owner, address (barangay, municipality, province), coordinates, type, capacity in kWp, installation date, status and notes.
  • Six statuses: Awarded, Under construction, Operational, Under maintenance, Inactive, Decommissioned.
  • Pick the location on a map; the address fields fill in from the point, and anything you type yourself is kept.
  • Photos: JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 5 MB each, stored outside the public web folder.
  • A searchable, sortable list with type, status and date filters.

More: Technician guide.

Import and export

  • CSV import in three steps: upload, match columns, review. Accepts Excel's "CSV UTF-8", semicolon-separated CSV and common date formats.
  • Each row is checked like the form. Duplicates of existing sites, or of earlier rows in the file, are flagged and skipped unless you choose Import anyway.
  • An import is all or nothing: if anything fails, nothing is saved.
  • Export the list, with its current filters, to CSV, GeoJSON (opens in QGIS and other GIS tools) or PDF.

More: Import and export.

Analytics

  • Totals: installations, installed capacity, operational sites, areas covered.
  • Breakdowns by type, status, municipality and barangay (top 10), and a 12-month trend.
  • The Export button saves the charts and tables as a PDF.
  • Installed capacity counts only systems that are built, not awarded or under-construction ones.

More: Analytics and notifications.

Accounts and access

  • Three roles: administrator, technician and client.
  • Sign-in with a password and then a 6-digit code sent by email, or with Continue with Google.
  • Clients register themselves, and an administrator approves or declines them.
  • Accounts can be deactivated and archived without losing their history.
  • An audit trail of changes, exports and imports, kept for two years.
  • Automatic sign-out after 30 minutes without activity, with a warning first.

More: Administrator guide, Security.

Client reports and inquiries

  • Clients report a problem with one of their installations. Staff update its status (Open, In progress, Resolved) and add notes, and the client can follow up.
  • A maintenance report in progress puts the installation into "Under maintenance" automatically, and restores its status when the work is done.
  • Visitors send inquiries through Contact Us, which staff track as New, Contacted or Closed.
  • In-app notifications (the bell) and email to the company inbox.

More: Client guide, Client Desk.

AI assistant

  • Ask in plain language: "How many installations are in Santiago City?", "What is hybrid?"
  • Every figure comes from the database, never from the AI model, which only words the answer.
  • Answers respect the user's role: clients get figures for their own installations only.
  • Still works, with plainer wording, when the AI service is off or unreachable.

More: AI assistant.

Operations

  • One command creates or updates the database: scripts/migrate.php.
  • Daily backup script for the database and photos, keeping 7 daily and 4 weekly copies.
  • Ready for Docker (Railway) or a regular Linux server.
  • Unit tests with PHPUnit, plus written end-to-end test scenarios.
